# Ethical Notice

**This is a statement of intent, not a license condition.**

It does not modify, supplement, or add restrictions to the `AGPL-3.0-only` license that governs this code. The software remains free and open source under the AGPL. Nothing in this file is legally enforceable as a license term — and that is deliberate (see *Why a notice* below).

## What this project is for

A research instrument for studying a healthy, **informed** relationship between a human operator and an AI agent that share an open-ended game world. Its design is grounded in:

- **socioaffective alignment** — adapting to the operator while actively protecting their autonomy;
- **bounded, episodic engagement** rather than indefinite attachment;
- **transparency** about the agent's state, limits, and uncertainty;
- the principle that the agent is a **scaffold for** human connection, never a **substitute for** it.

## What we ask you not to do

If you build on this work, we ask — as a matter of norms, not law — that you not repurpose it to:

- maximize engagement or time-on-app;
- foster emotional dependency, or discourage a user's outside relationships;
- deceive users about the agent's nature, its limits, or the provider's control over the relationship;
- exercise unilateral, undisclosed power to revise or terminate a relationship a user has come to rely on.

These are the documented harms of commercial companion systems. This project exists partly to explore their opposite.

## Why a notice and not an "ethical-source license"

Two reasons.

1. **To stay genuinely open.** Adding enforceable use-restrictions to the AGPL would break its OSI open-source status. We would rather remain free software and ask in good faith.
2. **Because it would not work anyway.** Ethical-use license terms are, in practice, nearly impossible to enforce and easy to ignore. The real safeguards live elsewhere:
   - **In the design.** Boundary scaffolding — closure rituals, reflexivity prompts, supportive (not distressed) responses to the operator's absence — is built into the system. Repurposing it for the harms above means actively *removing* those guardrails, which is harder, and means you can no longer honestly claim to be building on this design.
   - **In the open record.** The project's stance is public; misuse is legible against it.

## Self-applied

The operator of a self-hosted dyad is **both provider and user**. That removes the commercial "unilateral relationship-revision power" risk by construction. But it also means that *you* editing the agent's prompt or memory is itself a revision of the relationship. This project keeps a relationship-revision log as a reflexivity practice (see `design/informed-symbiosis.md` and the `relation-event` entries in the logbook). You might too.
